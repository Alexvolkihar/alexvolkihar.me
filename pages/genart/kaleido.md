Each piece starts in a single fundamental domain: a thin wedge, a triangle or a rectangle. A few flat shapes are dropped into it: petals and drops, lunes, arc bands, quarter and half discs, bars, stripes, stepped blocks and rows of dots. The largest go down first, some sit on a corner or along an edge, and some are nested in the shape before them, either in a second ink or cut out in the colour of the paper. Then the domain is reflected in its own edges, again and again, the way the mirrors of a kaleidoscope reflect the beads at the end of the tube. Two mirrors meeting at an angle of 180°/n give a rosette with n mirror lines. Concentric rings each get their own wedge and their own number of mirrors. Three mirrors forming a triangle with angles 45-45-90, 30-60-90 or 60-60-60, or four mirrors forming a rectangle, tile the whole sheet. These are the only four wallpaper groups generated by reflections alone, written \*442, \*632, \*333 and \*2222 in Conway's orbifold notation. Shapes that cross a mirror meet their own reflection and close into symmetric figures, and shapes that sit on a corner become medallions. Each piece is printed in three to five flat inks on a light or dark ground, and the print may carry a paper grain or a slight misregistration of the colour layers. Press **New seed** for a fresh hash, then share the URL to show someone that exact piece.

## Credits

- The kaleidoscope: Sir David Brewster, who invented and patented it in 1817 and described it in _A Treatise on the Kaleidoscope_ (1819). Two mirrors at an angle of 180°/n show the object between them reflected into a figure with n mirror lines.
- Symmetry groups: the dihedral groups of the rosettes and rings and the four planar reflection groups (kaleidoscopes) \*442, \*632, \*333 and \*2222, named in the orbifold notation of John H. Conway, Heidi Burgiel and Chaim Goodman-Strauss, _The Symmetries of Things_ (A K Peters, 2008). In crystallographic notation they are p4m, p6m, p3m1 and pmm. Every copy of the domain is found by reflecting it in its mirror edges, breadth first, until the canvas is covered.
- Rendering: each shape is built once as a vector path and filled in every copy, clipped to that copy of the domain. All copies of one shape are printed before the next shape, as a screen print lays one ink at a time. In the misregistered prints each ink layer is shifted on the sheet by 0.2 to 0.5% of the short side, clip included.
- Inspired by Camille Roux's [Bright Moments Finale](https://art.camilleroux.com/work/bright-moments-finale/) (2024). The titles, shapes, palettes and code here are original.
- Randomness: [sfc32](https://pracrand.sourceforge.net/) by Chris Doty-Humphrey. The hash seeding, named sub-streams and verification follow Camille Roux's [genart skill](https://github.com/camilleroux/genart-skill) (MIT).
- The palettes are original. The code was written with an AI assistant. It draws everything itself: no AI-generated images.
