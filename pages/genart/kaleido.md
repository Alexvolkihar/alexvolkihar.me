Each piece starts in a single fundamental domain: a thin wedge, a triangle or a rectangle. A few flat shapes are dropped into it: petals and drops, lunes, arc bands, quarter and half discs, bars, stripes, stepped blocks and rows of dots. The largest go down first, some sit on a corner or along an edge, and some are nested in the shape before them, either in a second ink or cut out in the colour of the paper. Then the domain is reflected in its own edges, again and again, the way the mirrors of a kaleidoscope reflect the beads at the end of the tube. Two mirrors meeting at an angle of 180°/n give a rosette with n mirror lines. Concentric rings each get their own wedge and their own number of mirrors. Three mirrors forming a triangle with angles 45-45-90, 30-60-90 or 60-60-60, or four mirrors forming a rectangle, tile the whole sheet. These are the only four wallpaper groups generated by reflections alone, written \*442, \*632, \*333 and \*2222 in Conway's orbifold notation. Shapes that cross a mirror meet their own reflection and close into symmetric figures, and shapes that sit on a corner become medallions. Each piece is printed in three to five flat inks on a light or dark ground, and the print may carry a paper grain or a visible misregistration of the colour layers. Press **New seed** for a fresh hash, then share the URL to show someone that exact piece.

## Credits

- The kaleidoscope: Sir David Brewster, who invented and patented it in 1817 and described it in _A Treatise on the Kaleidoscope_ (1819). Two mirrors at an angle of 180°/n show the object between them reflected into a figure with n mirror lines.
- Symmetry groups: the dihedral groups of the rosettes and rings and the four planar reflection groups (kaleidoscopes) \*442, \*632, \*333 and \*2222, named in the orbifold notation of John H. Conway, Heidi Burgiel and Chaim Goodman-Strauss, _The Symmetries of Things_ (A K Peters, 2008). In crystallographic notation they are p4m, p6m, p3m1 and pmm. Every copy of the domain is found by reflecting it in its mirror edges, breadth first, until the canvas is covered.
- Rendering: each shape is built once as a vector path and filled in every copy, clipped to that copy of the domain. All copies of one shape are printed before the next shape, as a screen print lays one ink at a time. Clips overlap their neighbours by a fraction of a pixel so that no seam shows on the mirrors. In the misregistered prints every ink but the key one is shifted on the sheet by 0.4 to 0.75% of the short side: each shape first knocks out bare paper where it belongs, then its ink is multiplied where it lands, which leaves a paper sliver on one side and a darker overprint on the other. Sparse pieces are checked at sample points and their shapes enlarged until at least half of the domain carries an ink that stands out from the ground.
- Inspired by Camille Roux's [Bright Moments Finale](https://art.camilleroux.com/work/bright-moments-finale/) (2024). The titles, shapes, palettes and code here are original.
- Randomness: [sfc32](https://pracrand.sourceforge.net/) by Chris Doty-Humphrey. The hash seeding, named sub-streams and verification follow Camille Roux's [genart skill](https://github.com/camilleroux/genart-skill) (MIT).
- The palettes are original. The code was written with an AI assistant. It draws everything itself: no AI-generated images.
