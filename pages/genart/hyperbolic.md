Put regular p-gons q to a vertex. On a flat plane this only closes up for squares, triangles and hexagons. In the hyperbolic plane any pair with (p − 2)(q − 2) > 4 works: seven triangles around a point, four pentagons, three octagons. Each piece builds the central tile of such a tiling {p,q} from its fundamental triangle, then reflects it across its edges until the tiles are too small to see. The edges are arcs of circles that meet the boundary at right angles. The plane is drawn in Poincaré's disk, centred on a tile, on a vertex or on some other point. It can also be drawn in the upper half-plane, where one tile hangs from the top over a cascade of smaller ones, or in the band model, a strip that runs the length of the canvas. The colourings respect the symmetry of the tiling. The triangles inside each tile can alternate, rings of tiles can alternate outwards, a pinwheel can turn a little further at each ring, or the triangles can swap colours from one ring to the next. The ornaments are abstract: stars, rings, petals, roundels, lenses and interlaced bands. They are built from hyperbolic circles, geodesics and equidistant curves, so every tile carries an exact copy. Press **New seed** for a fresh hash, then share the URL to show someone that exact piece.

## Credits

- Regular tilings and triangle groups: H. S. M. Coxeter, _Regular Polytopes_ (1948) and _Crystal Symmetry and Its Generalizations_ (Transactions of the Royal Society of Canada, 1957). Every tiling here is generated by reflections in the sides of his (2, p, q) triangle, and the alternating triangle colouring follows the black-and-white figures in his papers.
- Drawing them by computer: Douglas Dunham, John Lindgren and David Witte, _Creating Repeating Hyperbolic Patterns_ (SIGGRAPH 1981), and Dunham's later papers on hyperbolic symmetry and colour symmetry. As in their method, each tile is the image of the central one under a transformation of the disk, found by a breadth-first walk across edges. Here these are Möbius maps stored as 2×2 complex matrices.
- Models: Henri Poincaré's disk and half-plane (joined by the Cayley transform) and the band model, the image of the disk under the inverse hyperbolic tangent. Hyperbolic circles, geodesics and equidistant curves are all Euclidean circles in the disk, so each ornament is carried to every tile by mapping three points per arc.
- Interlace: the bands run along the complete geodesics through the edge midpoints. Each band passes over at the midpoint where it starts and under at the one where it ends, which makes the strands alternate along their whole length.
- The ornaments are original abstract shapes. The series contains no figures.
- Randomness: [sfc32](https://pracrand.sourceforge.net/) by Chris Doty-Humphrey, seeded from the hash with named sub-streams, following Camille Roux's [genart skill](https://github.com/camilleroux/genart-skill) (MIT).
- The code was written with an AI assistant. It draws everything itself: no AI-generated images.
